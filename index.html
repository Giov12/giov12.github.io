About

Overview

I am a bioinformatician who designs and builds software for the analysis of large biological datasets. I recently completed my Ph.D. in Biology at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where I developed tools for evaluating long-read genome assemblies and characterizing genome architecture at scale. My training began at Texas A&M University at Galveston, where I conducted phylogenomic research on tunas.

My doctoral work taught me what it takes to write good software: code that is fast, maintainable, and easy for other researchers to use. It also gave me the opportunity to work across a broad range of biological systems, including mammals, fishes, and plants, and across data types such as RNA-seq, whole-genome sequencing, RAD-seq, single-nucleus RNA-seq, and genome assemblies. The most important lesson from that breadth is that a good bioinformatician keeps biology at the forefront of every project. The software and the analysis exist to answer a biological question, not the other way around.

Key areas of expertise:

  • Genome assembly & evaluation: long-read assembly integrity and sequence-motif detection (Klumpy).
  • Comparative genomics: synteny-based characterization of genome architecture (Synolog).
  • Population genomics: marker discovery, population structure, and eQTL mapping.
  • Transcriptomics: single-cell and bulk RNA-seq analysis.
  • Cloud & HPC: AWS (EC2, S3, IAM) and Slurm.

Along the way I have collaborated with 8 research groups across departments and universities on projects spanning single-cell RNA-seq, eQTL mapping, genome assembly, and population genomics.
